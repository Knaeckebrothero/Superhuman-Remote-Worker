# critic-3

## verdict
The direction survives this review — the queue/lease substrate, journal-only streaming terminus, and deletion ledger are well-evidenced, and several numbers I recomputed do hold (queue-poll and heartbeat QPS are trivial, the per-persist fence lookup is negligible at ~10-25/turn, checkpoint growth under batching is genuinely unchanged since checkpoints are per-superstep regardless and keep-last-3 is orthogonal). But the doc's headline performance and cost figures are compositions presented as results, and the sharpest ones do not survive recomputation: the session lease-token/epoch "one counter" unification is self-contradictory in the doc's own SQL and, resolved either way, re-imports the per-turn client cascade or breaks monotonic fencing for the affinity common case; the S1 <2 s TTFT gate omits provider TTFT and rests on an in-process SSE wake that cannot work across the 2 orchestrator replicas the doc itself cites (and §2's "sub-second" is unachievable outright); the "≤25 INSERTs/s per 50 streams" journal figure is a ~100x understatement of the steady-state commit rate given the writer's own drain-what's-queued dynamics, and §5.5 never totals the load it adds to the single shared app PG; the 0.3-1 s per-turn core hides a repeat-every-claim aux-LLM summarization loop (Path A restore compacts but never persists the boundary — verified in code); and the <2% worker-overhead claim has no measured superstep denominator while its numerator excludes seconds-class MCP setup and the serialized dispatch handoff that its own cited research flags. None of this kills the design; all of it changes acceptance gates, the §5.2/§5.3.2 SQL, batch-budget definition (wall-clock, not supersteps), and interactive-fleet sizing, and should be corrected before S1 is gated on numbers the system cannot meet.

## findings (8)

### 1. [CRITICAL] §5.2 + 5.3.2
**Claim/gap:** "For sessions the token should *be* threads.events_epoch — one counter for SSE fencing and write fencing" contradicts the doc's own two SQL snippets: the §5.2 claim does `lease_token = lease_token + 1` unconditionally on every claim, while §5.3.2 requires `events_epoch + CASE WHEN lease_owner IS DISTINCT FROM $pod THEN 1 ELSE 0 END` (bump only on writer change).

**Evidence:** Verified textually in the doc (lines 100, 128, 142) and against the cascade cost the doc itself establishes. If the unified counter bumps per claim, every turn pays the §5.3.2 cascade the doc calls the single biggest S1 threat (~2.0–2.2 s dead polling + IndexedDB wipe + full-transcript refetch per turn — persistent-chat.service.ts:1628-1680 path). If it bumps conditionally, Kleppmann monotonic-per-acquisition fencing (§5.2's whole argument) is broken for the affinity common case: same-pod re-claim reuses the token, so leftover writers from claim N (the post-release background work §5.3.5 explicitly endorses) pass the fence during claim N+1. S1's acceptance ("epoch bumps ≤1 per steal, not per turn") asserts the conditional semantics; S1's build list ("fence") implies the unconditional one. One of the two snippets must be wrong.

**Fix:** Drop the one-counter unification. Keep lease_token as a per-claim unconditional fencing token (not client-visible, so per-turn bumps are free) checked by all persists including the journal batch CTE; keep events_epoch as the client-visible writer-generation counter bumped only on steal/rewind/takeover. State explicitly that sessions carry two counters with different bump rules, and adjust §5.2's last paragraph and the S1 build list accordingly.

### 2. [MAJOR] §9/S1 + 2 + 5.5
**Claim/gap:** S1 acceptance "create-to-first-token < 2 s" has no end-to-end budget and §2's "sub-second" is unachievable; the 200 ms SSE pin's wake mechanism fails on the doc's own 2-replica orchestrator.

**Evidence:** Verified by summation of the doc's own numbers plus code. Worst-case path: input POST accept/persist/enqueue ~30 ms → claim via deployment poll 250 ms–1 s (§5.1; the "nudge a pod directly" push has no pod-selection design — §7 deletes registration, so the orchestrator has no idle-pod map and a Service nudge round-robins onto busy one-turn-per-pod pods) → lite load 50–200 ms → provider TTFT 0.3–2 s+ (absent from every table in the doc — it is the dominant term) → journal write ~5 ms → SSE discovery 200 ms only if the poller was woken: the poll code (orchestrator/main.py, read: 200 ms ×5 → 1 s backoff) has no wake today, and the proposed "orchestrator receives the input POST — it can wake its own pollers; no NOTIFY infra needed" is an in-process signal, while §2 itself states dev runs 2 orchestrator replicas — the POST and the SSE stream for the same thread can land on different replicas, leaving the 1 s backoff in the TTFT path. Sum: ~0.85 s best case, ~4.3 s worst. §2's "sub-second" cannot survive the provider TTFT term alone.

**Fix:** Delete "sub-second" from §2 (or redefine it as create-to-turn-accepted). Publish an explicit TTFT budget line-itemed including provider TTFT, and specify the cross-replica wake: sticky SSE routing per thread, a threads.turn_inflight flag read on the existing 2 s epoch-recheck cadence, or a payload-free NOTIFY — then re-derive whether <2 s means <2 s-excluding-provider or the gate moves to <2.5–3 s.

### 3. [MAJOR] §5.5 + Appendix
**Claim/gap:** "≤25 INSERTs/s per 50 active streams — two orders below DBOS's measured 60 k/s ceiling" does not survive recomputation, and §5.5 never aggregates the total load it puts on the single shared app PG.

**Evidence:** Verified arithmetic. The ≤25/s figure assumes full 100-row batches, but the writer's own mechanics (one task per runtime, "batch drains whatever is queued", INSERT RTT 1–5 ms vs ~20–50 ms inter-token gap — persistent_app.py:3350-3597 per agent-3/agent-5 reports) produce ~1-row batches in steady state: statement/commit rate ≈ token-event rate ≈ 1.5–4 k commits/s at 50 streams, double at 100 — ~100× the stated figure, each commit a WAL flush. Even in row terms 2,500/60,000 = 24×, i.e. ~1.4 orders, not two. The unbudgeted read side at the doc's own scale: 100 streams pinned at 200 ms = 500 poll QPS plus the idle floor, plus fence SELECTs (~10–25/turn for sessions — mid-turn incremental message persists + journal batch CTEs; ~75/batch for workers at 3 checkpoint puts/superstep × 25), plus queue polls (pods × 1–4/s) and lease heartbeats (pods × 3/min, trivial). All of it lands on the one instance that also serves ~180 REST endpoints, jobs, thread_messages, and the D3 checkpoints; only vector and audit are separate. DBOS's 60 k/s was a dedicated benchmark box.

**Fix:** Replace the ≤25 INSERTs/s sentence with the honest regime statement: statement rate ≈ token rate (~2–4 k commits/s at 50–100 streams) with batching engaging only under backpressure; add a §5.5 aggregate-budget table (journal commits, SSE poll QPS, fence lookups, queue poll, heartbeat) vs a measured capacity number for the shared PG, and make "time-coalesce the writer flush (e.g. 25–50 ms tick) to force real batches" an explicit S1 tuning item — it converts the 100× gap back into the claimed regime for one poll-tick of added latency.

### 4. [MAJOR] §5.3.3 + 5.3.6
**Claim/gap:** The per-turn core "~0.3–1 s cold S2 / ~50–200 ms lite" omits an unbounded worst-case term: the Path-A restore runs ensure_within_limits(trigger="resume"), which invokes a blocking aux-LLM summarization when the loaded tail is over budget — and Path A never persists the resulting summary, so under per-turn reload the summarization repeats on every claim.

**Evidence:** Verified in code. persistent_app.py:5915-5928 (Path A) calls ctx_mgr.ensure_within_limits then only extends in-memory messages — no checkpoint write; only Path B "writes a checkpoint so the next resume hits Path A" (persistent_app.py:5956-5957, ~6017). context.py:1455-1467: ensure_within_limits → summarize_and_compact(auxiliary LLM) whenever should_summarize is true. The loop: claim N loads an over-threshold tail → aux-LLM summarize (seconds, plus aux token cost) → in-memory bound means the live path never triggers → no new role='summary' boundary row → claim N+1 reloads the same over-threshold tail and summarizes again. Today this costs once per rare attach; per-turn claims make it every turn indefinitely. Also worth an asterisk: token-recount on a ≤1000-row/≈395 k-token tail is hundreds of ms of CPU per claim (agent-5 A3), sitting at the top of the stated 0.3–1 s band before any compaction fires.

**Fix:** Add to §5.3.6's promote-before-S1 list: persist the resume-compaction summary as a role='summary' boundary row exactly as Path B does (or skip resume-time compaction and let the live path, which persists, handle it). Footnote the §5.3.3 core row: "worst case +1 aux-LLM summarization when tail-since-boundary is over budget; bounded to once per boundary only after the persist fix."

### 5. [MAJOR] §5.4.3 + 9/S3 + Appendix
**Claim/gap:** "Composed ≈0.5–1.5 s → <2% overhead at ≥25 supersteps" rests on an unmeasured denominator and an understated numerator; at the fast end it recomputes to 3–15%.

**Evidence:** Verified as unsupported plus internally inconsistent. (a) Denominator: no research report contains a measured superstep duration; 25 supersteps ≠ 25 LLM calls — the loop is execute→tools→check_todos plus phase bookkeeping (archive_phase/handle_transition/check_goal), so ~1/3 of supersteps are LLM calls: 25 supersteps ≈ 8–10 LLM calls ≈ 45–120 s wall with fast models, vs the 4–25 min the <2% needs. (b) Numerator: the doc's own sentence enumerates "full _setup_job_tools (MCP connect_all, KB/memory init, credential files)" inside the ≤1.5 s composition, while its source (agent-7 §2, agent-5 A1) calls MCP connect_all "network, seconds-class" and says the estimate "understates" per-claim cost — for MCP-attached jobs the claim alone is 2–5 s. (c) The serialized dispatch handoff (release → /complete → trigger → claim → /job/resume with credential re-injection ≈8–12 DB rt) adds ~1–5 s of dead time per batch and appears in no composition. Recomputed fast-end: (3–10 s)/(45–120 s) = 3–15% ≫ 2%. The S3 gate "batch overhead < 2–5%" then hinges on job mix, not on the design.

**Fix:** Define the batch budget by wall-clock, not superstep count (the doc already floats a wall cap for lease exposure — make it the primary knob, e.g. ≥5 min/batch ⇒ overhead <2% by construction). Restate the appendix row as "unmeasured; composed, excludes MCP-attached jobs and dispatch handoff" and make the Job Bench measure (i) per-claim setup split by MCP-attached vs not and (ii) release-to-next-claim handoff, before N is fixed.

### 6. [MAJOR] §5.6 + 9/S1
**Claim/gap:** The session-side KV-cache story is oversold: the "~10× input cost" figure is the ceiling not the marginal loss vs today, and the S1 acceptance "cache_read_input_tokens > 0 on turn 2" measures TTL/turn-timing, not the byte-determinism property under test.

**Evidence:** Partly verified in code, partly reasoning. (a) The repo already engineered the prompt head for cache stability: with_current_date is deliberately day-granularity "the system message is the head of the provider prompt-cache prefix, so a per-turn timestamp would invalidate that cache on every single turn" (loader.py:4242-4245, read) — so the headline nondeterminism risk is narrower than presented, and no baseline hit-rate is measured anywhere to compute a marginal. (b) Compaction rewrites the prefix identically today (summary replaces messages), zeroing the cache mid-thread stateful or stateless — the 10× loss on those turns is not attributable to this design. (c) For sessions, human inter-turn gaps routinely exceed Anthropic's 5 m TTL, so the turn-boundary cache is cold regardless of byte-stability; within-turn multi-call reuse is same-pod even in v1 (one claim spans the whole turn). The genuinely stateless-marginal exposure is fast follow-ups (<5 m) re-rendered from lossy rows — real, but narrow, and made worse by the doc's own §5.3.6 finding that restore drops thinking/structured content (a re-render can never be byte-identical until message fidelity lands, which S1 lists as merely a "decision"). (d) The turn-2 acceptance criterion passes in any rapid-fire bench and fails spuriously on any >5 m gap — it does not discriminate the design property. The 1 h-TTL lever (2× write per turn) is priced for workers only, never for sessions.

**Fix:** Reframe §5.6: byte-determinism is a worker-lane and fast-follow-up requirement whose marginal value for sessions is bounded by the 5 m TTL and by compaction cadence; make message fidelity (S1 Q5) a hard prerequisite of the determinism requirement rather than an independent decision; respecify the acceptance as "second LLM call within one turn/batch shows cache_read > 0, and a <5 m same-thread follow-up on a different pod shows cache_read > 0"; add a one-line cost comparison of ttl:1h for sessions.

### 7. [MINOR] §2
**Claim/gap:** "10 pods serve 30–50 sessions" ignores queueing at its own upper bound: 50 sessions × 20% duty = 10 concurrent turns offered to 10 one-turn-per-pod servers (ρ≈1 ⇒ unbounded waits); even 30×20% = 6 erlangs on 10 servers queues ~25–40% of turns, and any queued turn blows the S1 <2 s TTFT gate.

**Evidence:** Arithmetic on the doc's own numbers (duty cycle §2, one-turn-per-pod §8.3, TTFT gate §9). Sizing by mean utilization with zero headroom is incompatible with a latency acceptance criterion; Erlang-C at the advertised ratio's top end gives waits comparable to whole turn durations.

**Fix:** State the sizing as a latency-targeted formula (e.g. pods ≈ offered erlangs / 0.6–0.7 for P(wait) small), i.e. ~13–15 pods per 50 sessions at 20% duty — still a large win vs 50 pinned pods, and consistent with the TTFT gate; wire the KEDA/HPA floor for the interactive deployment to that target, not to average duty.

### 8. [MINOR] §Appendix + 9/S1
**Claim/gap:** "takeover ≤ ~90 s" (appendix and S1 acceptance "lease steals ≤ ~90 s") omits the reaper cadence: worst case is TTL 60 s + grace 30 s + reaper period 15 s + re-claim poll ≤1 s ≈ 106 s.

**Evidence:** Recomputation from the doc's own parameters (§5.2: TTL 60 s, steal at expiry+30 s, reaper every 15 s; §5.1 poll 250 ms–1 s). A pod dying immediately after a successful renewal reaches steal eligibility at +90 s but is only acted on at the next reaper tick.

**Fix:** Restate as ≤ ~105 s worst case (or shorten the reaper to 5 s to keep the 90 s number), and align the S1 acceptance wording so the soak test doesn't fail its own gate on a boundary-timed kill.
